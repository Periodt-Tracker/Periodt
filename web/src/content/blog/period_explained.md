Within a menstrual cycle, the lining of the uterus prepares for potential pregnancy, which begins when an egg is fertilised by sperm and implants into the lining of the uterus. If a fertilised egg does not implant into the uterine lining, the lining is shed, which is known as menstruation. 

The menstrual cycle is made up of two key phases, the follicular phase and the luteal phase. To understand these phases, we must first look into the hormones responsible for the menstrual cycle.

### Follicular Phase

The follicular phase begins on the first day of active bleeding (menstruation), and continues until ovulation.

#### Menstruation 

Menstruation is the the stage that your mind goes to when you think of a period. During menstruation, the uterine lining (made of blood and tissue) is shed through the vagina. Menstruation occurs if a fertilised egg has not been implanted into the uterine lining.

Menstruation usually lasts for around 5-7 days - if it lasts for longer than 7 days, it is considered 'prolonged menses'. 

During menstruation, you may use different period products to absorb or collect the blood released during your period. Make sure to carefully read through how often you have to change your period product to avoid health risks.

>[!info] Why change your period products so often?
> 
> Period products should be changed regularly to prevent infections, manage odurs and maintain comfort. 
> 
> Most importantly, not changing your period product regularly may be fatal in tampon and menstrual cup users. This is because these products can act as a growth medium for bacteria. When these bacteria release toxins in the vagina, this can lead to to [Toxic shock syndrome (TSS)](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/toxic-shock-syndrome/) - a rare but life-threatning condition. 
> - Symptoms of TSS include a high temperature, muscle aches, a raised skin rash and flu-like symptoms. Urgent medical care is needed if symptoms are present.

If you experience the following, it is advised to seek medical help:
- Prolonged menses (bleeding that lasts longer than 7 days)
- Going through a period product in less time than you are meant to, such as soaking through a pad or tampon every 1-2 hours
- Having to 'double up' and use multiple period products at once
- Passing blood clots larger than 2.5cm (around the size of a 10p coin or a quarter)
- Bleeding when not in your menstrual phase (also known as spotting)
- Symptoms of anemia during your period (fatigue, weakness, dizziness)
- Have to avoid daily activities because of your period
